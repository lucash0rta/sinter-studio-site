# Sinter Studio Design Notes

## Raw Notes From Whiteboard

- Make sure you know your industry and the industry knows you.
- Scan analytics, science-backed design, exportable brand/franchise thinking.
- Three core service lanes: 3D asset creation, liquid simulations, media.
- Target clients: who is the target, what is the vibe/tone?
- Site should cover: advice, maker, minimum viable reel.
- Early project inventory: 10 key references, readings, organic swag/clothing.
- Identity questions: why, ethos/ideology, how does the brand convey this?
- Brand system needs: key colours for in-house branding, logo, joint meeting with designer.
- Small design principles: 1px borders, no rounded edges, standardised 5px radius only if absolutely needed, PDF/brand kit.
- Web direction: video website, minimal visible copy, clear values in idea, personal relationship/in-person meetings.

## Strategy

Sinter Studio should feel like a precise digital workshop rather than a conventional agency site. The visual language should be sparse, technical, and a little industrial: thin rules, grids, hard edges, restrained typography, and a strong moving-image centerpiece.

The immediate website can stay intentionally minimal while the identity develops. The reel does the expressive work; the text should only state who Sinter is, where it is based, its construction status, LinkedIn, and members.

## Visual Guidelines

- Use hard rectangular framing, not capsules or soft cards.
- Prefer dotted or 1px borders over heavy filled components.
- Keep contrast high in dark mode and calm in light mode.
- Use limited copy and avoid explanatory marketing sections until positioning is clearer.
- Make the video the main artifact.
- Logo direction should explore grid-based, architectural, slightly hand-drawn forms.
- Avoid overly polished SaaS styling, rounded badges, gradients as decoration, and generic tech startup language.

## Draft Logo Direction

The first draft mark is a square grid monogram. It references the whiteboard sketch: a framed block, segmented internal paths, and an angular "S" movement across the structure. It should be treated as a rough foundation, not a final logo.
